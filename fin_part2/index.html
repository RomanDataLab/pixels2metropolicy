METROPOLITAN PLANNINGINTELLIGENCE PLATFORM

An interactive tool that joins several AI engines to predict, plan and carry out urban interventions in Thailand's metropolitan areas, with a focus on irregular neighborhoods. Modern cities assemble into complex, multilayered metropolises — de jure or de facto — where growing millions of people act every day, often with no plan, producing consequences that 20-year-old urban planning approaches cannot manage. Why does the metropolitan scale matter? Pedro B. Ortiz's MetroMatrix model gives three answers: urban growth no longer stops at municipal boundaries — the metropolis, not the municipality, is the real city; the metropolis is a single socio-economic system bound together by daily commuter flows, so managing it in fragments breeds sprawl and inefficiency; and it demands its own instruments — long-term structural vision and metropolitan governance — instead of static 20th-century master plans. Metropolitan and urban governors, planners and decision-makers can use this platform to deliver urban acupuncture: targeted interventions based on constantly updated satellite imagery and the world's best case studies of slum policies.
