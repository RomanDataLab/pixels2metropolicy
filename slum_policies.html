Slum policies — 16-country comparison

How 16 countries treated their largest informal settlements: what policy was written, what actually happened on the ground, and whether it worked. One representative case per country, with population estimates, build materials, main issues, the applied policy, and the de facto outcome. Brazil Rocinha favela, Rio de Janeiro
📍 Google Maps		 My Solo Footprints 150k–200k, under 2 km² Brick, concrete, multi-storey self-built No sanitation in parts, landslide risk, gang control, extreme density Favela-Bairro (1994), PAC Favelas (2007), Morar Carioca (2010) — in-situ upgrading [1][2] MixedWorks built, then abandoned. No maintenance, no continuous state policy. Programs restart under new names, stop again. Political crisis delays essential works; resident trust eroded after decades of neglect.
Colombia Comuna 13 + comunas 1–2, Medellín
📍 Google Maps		 Ecocity Builders Comuna 13 ~130k; comunas 1–2 ~300k+ Brick, block, self-built hillside housing Highest homicide rate in city (2004), disconnection from city, informality PUI Integral Urban Project (2004–2015), MetroCable, library parks — "social urbanism" [3][4] SuccessHomicides fell sharply, cable car + services connected the hills to the city, global model. Critique: gentrification pressure, displacement of poorest, maintenance gaps.
Kenya Kibera, Nairobi
📍 Google Maps		 Political Perspectives 250k official; up to 1M contested Mud walls, timber, corrugated tin sheets Extreme density, no tenure, "flying toilets", flooding, rent extraction by absentee structure-owners KENSUP / KSUP with UN-Habitat (2000s): Lang'ata decanting site, new apartment blocks [5][6] PartialOnly a few thousand rehoused. Decanting site disrupted social networks, ownership disputes + corruption stalled later phases. Most of Kibera remains as it was.
South Africa Khayelitsha township + ~1,500 targeted settlements, Cape Town
📍 Google Maps		 NSL – ETH Zürich Khayelitsha ~400k Corrugated-iron shacks, wood, cardboard Shack fires, floods, no services, apartheid spatial legacy, violence RDP free houses (1994), Breaking New Ground + UISP in-situ upgrading (2004), ring-fenced ISUP grant (2020) [7][8][9] Mixed3M+ free houses built; 84% formal housing now vs 65% in 1996. But RDP houses on far periphery replicate apartheid geography; beneficiaries sell or rent out and return informal. Delivery fell to 23k houses in 2025/26. Shift to upgrading is slow.
Tanzania Tandale, Manzese and citywide unplanned areas, Dar es Salaam
📍 Google Maps		 IIED ~70% of city informal, millions Mud + poles + iron sheets ("Swahili house"), sand floors No services, flooding, uncontrolled sprawl — informal areas grew 630% 1982–2022 20,000 Plots Programme (2002, delivered 36k+ plots), Residential Licences, MKURABITA titling, Citywide Upgrading Strategy (2007) [10][11][12][13] MixedPlots sold fast, costs recovered, titled land commands 71% price premium. But wealthier households bought plots, occupancy stayed low, speculation. Upgrading tiny vs growth — informal expansion continues anyway.
Gabon Akébé, Nombakélé, PK zones, Libreville
📍 Google Maps		 Jeune Afrique National slum pop ~790k (39% urban) Wood plank, earth, corrugated iron No services, tenure insecurity, demolition risk, decades of under-delivered housing State housing programmes (3,100 units Bikélé-Nzong + Essassa, 2026), déguerpissement clearances for roads, compensation + SNI plots [14][15][16] FailedHousing agencies under-delivered for decades (ANGT scandal: 60 funded schools never built). Clearances continue; compensation judged too small, rehousing weak, residents protest.
Angola Musseques (Sambizanga etc.), Luanda
📍 Google Maps		 Dezeen ~8M in musseques Mud block, zinc sheets, sand floors No water or sanitation, extreme occupancy, illegal on paper, demolition threat Tabula rasa: demolish central musseques, build Chinese "centralidades" new towns (Kilamba etc., 2007+), rehouse on outskirts [17][18] FailedTop-down, zero consultation; master plan implied rehousing 5.9M people (86% of Luanda). Kilamba flats unaffordable at first; now 65% of Kilamba and Sequele residents in rent debt. Musseques keep growing.
Zambia Kanyama, George, Garden compounds, Lusaka
📍 Google Maps		 Unequal Scenes Kanyama ~250k+; 1973: 1 in 2 Lusaka residents a squatter Mud block, pole-and-mud, iron sheets Cholera outbreaks (2017–18, 2023–24), flooding, no drainage, density World Bank squatter upgrading + sites-and-services (1970s: 17k dwellings serviced, 7,600 new plots) — among the world's first large in-situ programmes [19][20][21] Early success, stalled1970s upgrading worked where implemented and became a global model. Then cost recovery and standards killed scale. Today over 50% of urban Zambia is informal and cholera returns to Kanyama.
Namibia Katutura + extensions (Havana, Otjomuise), Windhoek
📍 Google Maps		 EnergyTransition.org Tens of thousands of shack households, fast growth Corrugated-zinc shacks, wood No services, multi-year land waitlists, tenure insecurity Informal Settlement Upgrading Policy (1999): block-by-block; Shack Dwellers Federation (SDFN) co-production, saving groups buy and service own land; PISUP with UN-Habitat [22][23] Partial successCo-production praised globally; SDFN delivered affordable land to thousands. But backlog grows faster than delivery, and poorest cannot afford even serviced plots. State now funds mass land servicing.
India Dharavi, Mumbai
📍 Google Maps		 Citizen Matters 700k–1M on 2.4 km² Brick + concrete + tin, dense mixed residential-industrial Highest density in the city, housing mixed with industry, no tenure, pollution SRA model (1995): developer gets sale FSI, builds free flats for dwellers; Dharavi Redevelopment Project (2004), Adani survey (2024) [24] Stalled 20 yearsEligibility cutoff disputes, resident and business resistance; only a fraction rehoused. SRA citywide delivered flats but small units, corruption reports, livelihoods lost when workshops move. Redevelopment still contested.
UAE Sonapur / Al Muhaisnah labor camps, Dubai
📍 Google Maps		 Labor Accommodation Dubai Millions of migrant workers nationally; camps 1k–8k each Portacabin and prefab bunk dormitories, company-built Overcrowding, extreme heat, wage abuse, fire risk, no family life possible No slums tolerated: employer-must-house rule, Ministerial Resolution 766/2015 minimum standards, inspections, fines and closure, wage protection system [25][26] Contained, not solvedTop-tier worker villages good; bottom-tier subcontracted camps still bad (ventilation failures, permit suspensions). Workers can never settle or bring families. The problem is hidden rather than solved.
Malaysia Desa Mentari PPR + former kampung setinggan, Kuala Lumpur / Selangor
📍 Google Maps		 Rice Media Tens of thousands of households relocated Former squatter housing: wood + zinc; replacement: high-rise concrete flats Open dumping, floodplain occupation, stigma Zero Squatter (Setinggan Sifar): Selangor 2001, target 2005; demolition + relocation to PPR low-cost high-rise; PPRT programme 1980s+ [27][28][29] Cleared, quality poorSquatter settlements mostly cleared by mid-2000s and the target declared met. But PPR flats meet minimum standards only — bad design, weak facilities, social problems (Desa Mentari study). Hidden squatting persists; states re-pledged zero squatter in 2014.
Singapore Bukit Ho Swee kampong (historic), Singapore
📍 Google Maps		 Rice Media 1961: 16k homeless in one night; squatters a large city share Attap thatch, wood, zinc kampong dwellings Fires 2–3 times a year, no sanitation, congestion HDB public housing (1960), Land Acquisition Act (1966), mass resettlement, 99-year-lease flats sold via CPF [30][31] Success — global benchmark1961 fire became the push: victims rehoused within a year, then rolling resettlement. Squatters gone by the 1980s. ~80% of residents live in HDB, ~90% homeownership. Cost: kampong community and culture erased.
China Baishizhou urban village, Shenzhen
📍 Google Maps		 The World of Chinese 150k in ~0.6 km²; urban villages house 10M+ citywide "Handshake" brick-concrete blocks 7–8 floors, villager-built Fire risk, density, poor sanitation — yet the city's only affordable migrant housing Urban village redevelopment: 2019 demolition + LVGEM rebuild, big landlord compensation; hukou blocks migrant access to city services; 2023 national urban-village renovation push [32][33] In progress — displacementDemolition ongoing since 2019; media counted 1,878 landlord millionaires. Migrants lose housing: 28% of Baishizhou residents would leave Shenzhen, 51% face longer commutes. Vanke's no-demolition rental plan was abandoned.
Spain Cañada Real Galiana, Madrid
📍 Google Maps		 El Salto ~8,000 (4,500 in the cut-off sectors); 39% Roma Shacks, improvised brick/block, caravans, some solid houses No legal electricity for 4+ years (cut 2020), no tenure, drug market in sector 6, evictions 2017 closure + rehousing plan for sector 6; inter-ministerial group (2021); social housing promises; partial power restoration [34][35][36][37] Failed so farEuropean Committee of Social Rights ruled (Feb 2025) Spain violates the Charter — no decent housing, no electricity. Rehousing slow, families wait years. Largest shantytown in Western Europe persists.
Mexico Ciudad Nezahualcóyotl (Neza), Mexico City metro
📍 Google Maps		 EL PAÍS 1.1M–1.5M on 25 km² of drained lakebed Self-built cinderblock + concrete; early phase cardboard and metal Decades without water/sewer, lakebed flooding and sinking, poverty, crime Regularization, not demolition: land titles, municipality created 1963, service investment 1970s–80s, CORETT/PASPRAH/PRAH title programmes, Piso Firme cement floors (2007–13, US$1.27B national) [38][39][40][41][42] SuccessNeza became a formal municipality with services, consolidated into the metro area — a textbook regularization model. Piso Firme delivered proven health gains in a randomized evaluation. Residual pockets and title disputes remain.

Cross-country patterns

  • In-situ upgrading (Medellín, Lusaka 1970s, Namibia): cheapest per household, keeps communities and jobs. Needs 20-year maintenance commitment. Brazil shows what happens without it.
  • Demolition + peripheral relocation (Angola, Malaysia, China): clears land fast, breaks livelihoods, creates debt traps (Kilamba: 65% rent arrears) and poor-quality towers (Desa Mentari).
  • Mass public housing (Singapore): the only proven full-elimination case. Required a strong state, land acquisition law, sale rather than rent, and 30 years of sustained construction.
  • Regularization + titles (Mexico, Tanzania): turns a slum into a city slowly. Tanzania shows titles alone favor cash buyers and feed speculation.
  • Free houses (South Africa): big numbers, wrong locations, resale leakage, delivery decay.
  • Do nothing / contain (Spain, UAE): court rulings for violations, hidden workforce camps, problem persists.
