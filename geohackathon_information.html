Background and Significance

Background and Significance

In an era where Southeast Asia is rapidly digitizing, the key challenge is no longer merely accessing data. A critical gap is the ability to transform these raw pixels into deep, timely, and spatially relevant insights that can keep pace with the region's complex challenges.

GeoHackathon 2026 is designed to be a platform for visionaries from diverse fields. Under the theme "Pixel to Policy: Unlocking Insights, Accelerating Impact," We challenge participants to harness the power of GeoAI—the synergy of Geospatial Science and Artificial Intelligence. The goal is to convert spatial data into Actionable Geo-Intelligence that addresses urgent public issues, from climate resilience and urban planning to resource management and social equity.

We are looking for solutions that bridge the gap between sophisticated analytical outcomes and real-world decision-making. While the solutions should be applicable to the unique socio-economic landscape of Thailand and the broader Southeast Asian region, the impact of your work should demonstrate a scalable model for evidence-based policy globally. We, therefore, invite developers, students, researchers, programmers, startups, and members of the public to demonstrate their potential through solutions that create a positive, and data-driven impact that shapes the future of our societies.

Objectives

Objectives

1. To promote the development of Actionable Geo-Intelligence insights that support evidence-based policy decision-making in the regional context.

2. To build and expand a community of developers, researchers, and analysts with expertise in GeoAI.

3. To connect academia, government, and the private sector through collaborative spatial problem-solving.

4. To develop the country's capacity to use satellite data and AI for sustainable planning and national management.

Competition Challenge

Competition Challenge

Unified Challenge Framework
Participating teams must use geospatial data together with AI/ML methods or GeoAI to develop Actionable Geo-Intelligence that clearly addresses a problem in the regional context. The work should reflect an end-to-end process covering: problem definition, data selection and preparation, analysis or model development, spatial output creation, and explanation of how the results can be applied in practice.

Teams may select a challenge from the following domains, or propose their own issue within the GeoAI and regional context framework:

● Food security and agricultural monitoring
● Urban expansion and informal settlement (slum communities)
● Flood risk and disaster preparedness
● Forest degradation and carbon stock estimation
● Inter-provincial development disparities
● Coastal change and fisheries
● Other topics proposed by the team, within the GeoAI and Regional context framework

Sample Datasets

Sample Datasets for the Competition

Participants may use data from the provided datasets or other appropriate open data. Recommended datasets include:

● Sentinel-1/2 (ESA Copernicus) — SAR and Optical satellite imagery
● THEOS-2 imagery (GISTDA)
● VIIRS Nighttime Light data
● OpenStreetMap and Thai administrative boundaries
● ERA5 Climate Reanalysis
● Socioeconomic data from the World Bank or National Statistical Office (NSO)

Teams may also procure or create additional open datasets as appropriate, and must clearly cite all data sources in the project proposal and final presentation.

Target Participants and Eligibility

Target Participants and Eligibility

Open to all groups including students, researchers, software developers, programmers, policy analysts, startups, and interested members of the public — with no restrictions on gender, age, or educational background.

Participants must form teams of 3–5 members. We particularly encourage cross-disciplinary teams that include:

● Members with technical skills such as GIS, Remote Sensing, Data Science, or Machine Learning
● Members with policy knowledge or domain expertise in the selected topic

Diverse team composition strengthens the solution's completeness — technically, analytically, and in real-world applicability.

Competition Format

Competition Format

The competition is divided into two phases:

Phase 1

Selection Round

Applicants must submit a registration form and project proposal (Proposal-based Selection) for the committee to review and select approximately 10–12 teams with strong potential.

Phase 2

Development and Final Round

Selected teams begin developing their projects from August 2026 onward, supported by Mentoring Sessions, before presenting their work in the Final Round on 31 October 2026 at the Thailand Space Expo 2026.

Final Pitching: 31 Oct 2026 at Thailand Space Expo 2026

Judging Criteria

Selection Round (based on project proposal)

The committee — comprising experts from GISTDA, academic institutions, and partner organisations — will evaluate applications on a total of 100 points:

Points

What is evaluated

Soundness of proposed GeoAI approach30Appropriateness and feasibility of the AI/ML methodology and data selection; logical and technically sound approach
Team capability and cross-disciplinary diversity20Team composition and skills aligned with the project; clear member roles; realistic capacity to deliver
Feasibility of output and real-world applicability 20Clear and achievable expected output; potential to become a tool, insight, or policy recommendation with practical use
