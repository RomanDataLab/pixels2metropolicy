Participation techniques

The participation pool behind stage 4 of the decision pipeline: one card per technique, extracted from the world's slum policies review, classified against two standard frames (Arnstein's ladder, 1969; the IAP2 spectrum: inform / consult / involve / collaborate / empower), and extended with techniques that exist and operate in Thailand today. Card colour = class; the TH tag marks Thai institutions; KIT n marks which policy kit on the proposal page uses it. Filter by class or drag/scroll the carousel.

Failure modes — why "participation" alone is not a technique

  • Voice without empowerment. Kibera's dialogue forums were dominated by absentee structure-owners: participation written into the project design, power not.
  • Participation cut mid-programme. The iBase-contracted participatory meetings in Rio ended in 2013; resident trust followed them out.
  • Token timing. Consultation held after the budget is locked — class 1 dressed as class 2.
  • Elite capture. The loudest committee, not the most exposed households, sets the works sequence.
  • Expectation debt. Announcing participation and then stalling works (Mathare NYS, Nairobi) — participation raises the cost of abandonment.
